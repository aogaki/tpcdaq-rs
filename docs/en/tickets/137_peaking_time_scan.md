> **Correction (night of 2026-10-14, tested in 139)**: the statement below, "charge goes 1 → 2.3 → 5.2 times with peaking … the current 232 ns integrates only part of the signal", is wrong. The charge here is a **sum of samples**, and for the same input charge it grows in proportion to the area of the shaped pulse (sums of the kernels 9.7 / 20.7 / 40.3, archive/138). Deconvolving with the 138 kernels brings 232 : 502 : 1014 to 1 : 1.05 : 1.21 (= the pulser peak gain ratio) → the total charge is fully collected at every peaking. What really changes with peaking is the peak height (S/N, threshold crossing, saturation) and the time width.
- **Charge goes 1 → 2.3 → 5.2 times with peaking** (232 → 502 → 1014). Because the charge arrival in the detector is µs wide, a short shaping cannot collect the full amplitude (ballistic deficit). **The current 232 ns integrates only part of the signal**. At 70 ns weak strips fall below the threshold (35 ADC / STRIP_Q_MIN 2000), ok 66 %, length 45 mm = a big loss as tracks (the eyeballed "discrimination is good" is the appearance of only the bright strips remaining).
- The longer the shaping, the more weak strips are picked up (hit ch 121 → 147), the ends of the track extend (85 → 105 mm), and the relative width of dEdx shrinks (0.52 → 0.43).
- Price: **saturation** (1/3 of events have a ch at 4095 at 1014 ns, 4.8 % at 502 ns) and **time width** (pulse width 1.6 → 2.5 → 4.2 µs = z resolution and window occupancy).
